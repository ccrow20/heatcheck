      How the heat index works

      Every morning Heat Check pulls the previous night's box scores and compares each player's last 5 games with his usual level: the 30 games before those, reaching back into last season when needed.

      The heat index measures how unusual that difference is for that player. A scorer who swings between 12 and 35 points needs a bigger jump to register than one who scores 18 every night. An index of +2 means a stretch that good would come up only about 2% of the time for him by chance. Players at +1.5 or higher are heating up; −1.5 or lower, cooling off.

      Games under 10 minutes don't count, and a player needs 13 qualifying games, with one in the last 10 days, to be ranked. To be called cold he has to normally produce (10 points a game for points, for example), so a bench player who goes quiet doesn't crowd out a struggling star.

      Game Score is John Hollinger's one-number summary of a box score: points, plus credit for rebounds, assists, steals and blocks, minus missed shots, turnovers and fouls.

      Box scores from ESPN, with API-NBA as a backup. Not affiliated with the NBA or its teams.
